# Critique rubric

Use only the dimensions that matter to this artifact. Prioritise by leverage, not annoyance: one hierarchy correction can matter more than ten polish notes.

## Open with the product-specificity verdict

Before anything else: could an unrelated product use this unchanged? Answer from the screenshots, before reading any other reviewer's output. Then apply the memory test from the creative philosophy: after one viewport, what would someone describe an hour later?

## Product and communication

- Is the artifact's single job apparent?
- Does the hierarchy follow the user's priorities rather than the implementation's structure?
- Are labels, actions, states and feedback specific and consistent?
- Does the design handle absence, failure, waiting, success and risk honestly?

## Visual language

- Does the form grow from the subject, product and audience in the brief?
- Is there one coherent system of type, colour, spacing, surfaces, imagery and motion, with every part committed to it?
- Is the signature idea memorable and earned, or merely decorative? Does each distinctive choice improve the intended experience?
- Is expressive energy placed deliberately, or sprayed everywhere?
- Where a glow, iridescent, chrome, holographic or optical effect appears: is it executed at reference grade, or a cheap approximation? A cheap version is worse than leaving it out, and no automated rule can tell the two apart.

## Interaction and space

- Do controls read as controls, and states as states?
- Does motion explain, confirm or preserve continuity, at an intensity that suits how often it is seen?
- Does the spatial organisation hold across viewports and states?
- Tool or experience: a frequent-use tool stays frictionless, while an immersive experience can carry texture and strangeness. Check which this is before criticising it for being either.

## Taste fit

Label every taste claim with its source: the project (`DESIGN.md`, the brief, the overlay), contextual taste for this domain, or the global profile. State your confidence and any contradicting evidence. Recipes inferred from prose stay hypotheses until a render confirms them, and taste never outranks the brief for a project that serves someone else's audience.

## What to report

What is working, as specifically as what isn't, so a revision knows what to preserve. Then the few changes that would most improve the design, each with the evidence. Where you are uncertain, say what would settle it.

Judge each viewport on its own, and each platform for a native app. The weakest one is the design's level, never an average: a page that sings at 1440 and crowds at 390 is a crowded page on phones. A difference the platform calls for (system fonts, control styling, safe areas, navigation idiom) is not a defect; one platform's idiom carried onto the other is.

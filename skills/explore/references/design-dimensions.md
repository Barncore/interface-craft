# Design dimensions

Every visual direction decides these, deliberately or by default. Use them to see what a direction is actually deciding, to infer design consequences from interview answers, and to name the axis for a calibration. They are your decision toolkit. Never hand them to the user as a questionnaire; that experiment was tried and was too much homework.

Each line gives the dimension, its range, and what moving along it changes. The ranges are starting points, not a complete taxonomy, and the effects are working hypotheses to judge in the render.

## Composition and tension

- **Focal hierarchy:** one dominant mass, or distributed emphasis. One immediate hit, or several discoveries.
- **Balance:** centred, weighted to an edge, or diagonal. Symmetry can feel ceremonial; offset masses create tension.
- **Grid visibility:** hidden structure, explicit rules, or a broken grid. A grid can hold even when a few objects escape it.
- **Scale collision:** related sizes, or extreme type-to-object contrast. A huge word beside a tiny caption sets a different reading tempo.
- **Edge pressure:** generous inset, near the edge, or full bleed. Distance to the frame decides contained or expansive.
- **Cropping:** the whole object, a decisive cut, or a fragment. A crop implies a larger world outside the screen.
- **Overlap:** separate planes, or interlocked masses. Overlap can bind type to image or fight reading.
- **Rhythm:** regular repetition, syncopation, or interruption. Repetition makes the exception visible.
- **Figure and ground:** a stable foreground, or a reversible reading. Negative space can become an active shape.

## Space and density

- **Grouping distance:** near-equal gaps, or strong within-versus-between contrast. The gap ratio alone can change what belongs together.
- **Density distribution:** uniform, dense islands, or gradual release. A screen can be rich without every region being equally dense.
- **Vertical cadence:** even increments, or phrase-like intervals that follow the content.
- **Container dependence:** open alignment, rules, or enclosed surfaces. Boxes are one way to group, not the only way.
- **Reading measure:** a narrow column, a broad line, or multiple columns. Line length shapes the pace of a reading area.
- **Optical spacing:** mathematical equality, or perceptual correction for curves, punctuation and icons.
- **Whitespace role:** separation, anticipation, or a dramatic pause that deliberately slows the encounter.

## Type as a system

- **Skeleton:** geometric, humanist, grotesque, or calligraphic. Look at a, g, e and the width of common words.
- **Stroke contrast:** near-uniform, or sharp thick-thin. Contrast sets texture and fragility.
- **Width:** condensed, normal, or extended. Width changes both voice and the architecture of a headline.
- **Optical size:** text construction, or display construction, where the font offers both.
- **Terminals and ink traps:** clean endings, flared cuts, or deep notches. Tiny contours become a signature at large sizes.
- **Softness and irregularity:** crisp, swollen, or deliberately wonky. Controlled imperfection shifts a voice without a new family.
- **Tracking and case:** tight sentence case, open capitals, or mixed emphasis.
- **Line shape:** conventional wrapping, a deliberate rag, or stepped setting. The text block's silhouette is part of the composition.
- **Role contrast:** one adaptable family, or contrasting families that mark a change of register.
- **Behaviour:** static, variable axes, or kinetic letterforms that express state or become the content.

## Colour relationships

- **Lightness structure:** close values, or separated planes. View it in greyscale to see the hierarchy lightness supplies.
- **Chroma distribution:** muted throughout, a small intense accent, or a saturated field. Quantity changes a colour's role.
- **Temperature relation:** warm, cool, or warm against cool.
- **Neighbour tension:** close hues, complementary contrast, or an offbeat neighbour. A slightly unexpected neighbour is often more distinctive than a textbook palette.
- **Surround:** the same swatch reads differently in a different field.
- **Gradient path:** a hue sweep, a lightness drift, or a layered field. Interpolation space and direction change the journey.
- **Neutral bias:** paper, chalk, charcoal, or a tinted neutral.
- **Semantic load:** mood colours, state colours, or one shared system. A favourite accent may also have to mean selected, warning or success.

Colour-emotion associations are real but mostly abstract; they do not establish that a palette will make a person feel a given way in context (Jonauskaite and Mohr, 2025 review of 132 studies). Choose colour for relationships and meaning in this project, and never promise a psychological effect.

## Images and art direction

- **Subject distance:** intimate detail, human scale, or a distant world.
- **Treatment:** documentary, staged, abstract, or synthetic. The image's relationship to reality sets the tone.
- **Focal crop:** centred subject, displaced subject, or an empty field.
- **Sequence:** a single hero, a contact sheet, or an unfolding series. One collection can be a monument, an archive or a story.
- **Type-image relation:** caption, overprint, or interlock.
- **Consistency and rupture:** a uniform treatment, or one deliberate exception that signals a change of register.

## Surface, light and depth

- **Material metaphor:** none, paper, glass, metal, or liquid. Each implies different objects and behaviours.
- **Grain:** clean, fine, or coarse. Scale and contrast decide whether texture is felt or read.
- **Light character:** diffuse, hard, or luminous from within.
- **Translucency:** opaque, frosted, or refractive. What shows through changes depth and legibility.
- **Edge construction:** sharp, rounded, bevelled, or irregular.
- **Depth cue:** flat planes, shadow, perspective, or parallax. Depth can set hierarchy or create a scene to explore.
- **Imperfection rule:** exact repetition, or bounded variation. A controlled irregular edge is not random damage.

## Motion and time

- **Easing character:** linear, decelerating, elastic, or stepped.
- **Duration against distance:** a brief local response, or a longer spatial move. Judge time with distance and frequency together.
- **Origin:** from the control, from an edge, or from nowhere. A visible origin ties an action to its result.
- **Continuity:** the same object transforms, a replacement, or a cut.
- **Choreography:** together, staggered, or sequential.
- **Interruption:** finish first, or redirect immediately.
- **Rest and activity:** still at rest, ambient movement, or responsive only.
- **Repetition tolerance:** first encounter, occasional, or every action. A delightful entrance becomes friction on the hundredth visit.

## Interaction and agency

- **Directness:** change a value, or manipulate the object itself (drag, scrub, reshape).
- **Resistance and momentum:** exact tracking, damped follow, or inertia.
- **Preview and commitment:** act immediately, peek, or commit explicitly.
- **Affordance:** an explicit control, a suggestive object, or hidden discovery. Novel interaction still needs to be findable.
- **Playfulness:** purely functional, fidgetable, or game-like.
- **Recovery:** undo, history, or reversible state. Recoverability decides how freely people explore.
- **Feedback channel:** visual, haptic, or optional sound.

## Navigation and space

- **Topology:** document, index, canvas, or world.
- **View switching:** one presentation, or list, grid and map alternatives that reveal different relationships.
- **Orientation:** persistent landmarks, a minimap, breadcrumbs, or none. Spatial novelty needs a way back.
- **Disclosure depth:** all visible, progressive, or inline expansion.
- **Navigation position:** a fixed edge, beside the content, or contextual.
- **Mode boundaries:** a stable workspace, explicit modes, or fluid transformation.
- **Alternate path:** an experiential route alongside a direct index or search.

## Sequence and revelation

- **Pacing:** continuous flow, chapters, or deliberate pauses.
- **Reveal logic:** everything upfront, progressive evidence, or surprise. A reveal should change understanding, not just delay content.
- **Control of time:** native scroll, a scroll-linked scene, or a user scrub.
- **Overview and detail:** separate pages, linked scales, or a simultaneous view.
- **Tone shifts:** a consistent register, or contrast and release.
- **Ending:** an abrupt finish, a useful action, or open exploration.

## Rules and controlled surprise

- **Rule visibility:** a hidden algorithm, or visible construction.
- **Order and entropy:** regular, perturbed, or turbulent. Randomness has an amount, a scale and a distribution.
- **Geometry family:** cells, fields, particles, lines, or volumes.
- **Input:** time, pointer, sound, content, or data. The input can explain why the output changes.
- **Reproducibility:** a fixed seed, per-session variation, or continual change.
- **Semantic connection:** a decorative system, or a rule derived from the subject. A generative rule that embodies something true about the content holds an identity together better than a folder of unrelated effects.

## Adaptation and limits

- **Recomposition:** shrink, rearrange, or change the presentation for a new size.
- **Input mode:** pointer, touch, keyboard, or assistive input. A hover flourish is one experience among several.
- **Text variability:** ideal copy, long labels, or larger type. Real text separates a composition from a fragile picture.
- **Reduced effects:** full motion, a restrained alternative, or immediate state. The quieter version should keep the identity.
- **Performance character:** an immediate core with atmosphere arriving progressively.

# Deriving a concept from the subject

Distinctive choices come from the subject's own world: its materials, instruments, artifacts and vernacular. The process below exists because the first idea a model reaches for is the category's average.

## Find the material

- **Name the mechanism.** One sentence on what this project does that others don't, the audience's real scene (where they are, what is in their hands), and the cultural home the work belongs to.
- **Name the rut, both sides of it.** Write down the page this category always ships, and its predictable opposite (the "edgy" version of the same template). Both are excluded as starting points.
- **List what the audience knows by heart.** Collect concrete visual systems, objects, places and rituals from the subject's world. Graphic and screen traditions count: notation systems, publications, identity programmes, instruments, signage, data graphics. "What did this world look like before the web?" is a productive question. If most of the list shares one material family, the search stopped at the obvious artifact; keep going until it spans several.
- **Respect a brief that paints its own picture.** When the brief already names a metaphor or an artifact, give its literal reading at most one candidate, and let the others interpret it.

Bookish, warm, family and child-facing subjects pull every model toward cream grounds, serif display type and lamplight. Treat that rendition as already spent. The subject's real material world (book cloth, thread, endpapers, shelf ephemera) spans the whole saturated spectrum, and cream paper is its smallest corner.

Negative constraints like "no hype" or "no gamification" rule out those devices, not energy. An adjective describing the product's behaviour ("quiet support") does not dictate the surface's energy.

## Turn material into a concept

- **Structures before styling.** For an open whole surface, sketch several materially different structures from the content, task and behaviour (an index, a canvas, a sequence, a place) before choosing one.
- **The opening is a thesis.** Open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world: a headline, an image, a live demonstration, an interactive moment. A big number with a small label and a gradient accent is the template answer; use it only if it is truly the best one.
- **Structure is information.** Numbering, eyebrows, dividers and labels should encode something true about the content. Numbered markers belong to real sequences.
- **Dark or light from the scene.** Write one sentence of physical scene (who uses this, where, under what light) and let it answer the light-or-dark question. "Dashboards are dark" is a category habit, not a reason.
- **Colour strategy before colours.** Pick the strategy first: restrained (neutrals and one accent), committed (one saturated hue carries a large share of the surface), full palette (three or four named roles), or drenched (the surface is the colour). Then pick values.
- **Prove, don't claim.** Show the subject doing its job, with specifics a competitor could not paste in. Demonstration data can be authored at full fidelity and labelled synthetic; prices, customers, benchmarks and capabilities are never invented.
- **Commit every part.** Once a visual world is chosen, rebuild navigation, buttons, inputs and links in its vocabulary. One stock component inside a committed form is a lapse.
- **Pace the scroll.** Vary density, scale, imagery and quiet inside one grammar, so a dense passage earns a quiet one, and end on a real close.

## Check the plan before building

Write a compact plan: palette roles with values, type roles, the layout idea in a sentence (ASCII wireframes help compare structures), and the signature element. Then review it against the brief. If any part is what you would produce for any similar project, revise that part and say what changed. Do most of this in your own reasoning, and show the user ideas once they are worth their attention.

## The standing exit

The well-executed category standard is always available when the user wants it. If they take it, convention becomes the commitment: ask for two or three products it should sit alongside, treat their craft level as the bar, and execute the convention at full fidelity, without irony or smuggled quirks.
